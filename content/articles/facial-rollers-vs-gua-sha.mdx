Facial rollers and gua sha stones are two of the most recognizable tools on a modern vanity. Both are used to massage the face, both come in stone and metal, and both are surrounded by bold claims. They're also genuinely different to use. Here's how to compare them, and what the evidence does and doesn't support.

## The short difference

A **facial roller** is a handle with one or two small cylinders that roll across the skin. It's intuitive: you can't really hold it at the wrong angle.

A **gua sha** tool is a flat, smooth-edged stone or metal piece that's glided across the skin. Its different edges and curves are designed for different areas of the face, which makes it more versatile — and more dependent on technique.

## What the research found

Evidence on facial tools is limited, but there is at least one randomized controlled trial comparing the two directly. Published in 2025 in the *Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology*, it [assigned 34 women aged 20 to 50](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40439289/) to use either a facial roller or a gua sha tool for 10 minutes, five times a week, for eight weeks.

The researchers reported different short-term changes for each group: the gua sha group showed small reductions in facial contour measurements and changes in muscle-tone measures, while the roller group showed changes in skin elasticity measures.

<Callout title="How to read this study" tone="caution">
  This is a small trial — about 17 people per group — that ran for eight weeks and compared the two tools with each other rather than with a no-treatment group. It suggests the tools may produce measurable short-term changes, but it can't show whether effects last after you stop, or how a specific tool you buy would perform. Treat claims of permanent sculpting or "lifting" with skepticism.
</Callout>

## Materials: stone or steel?

Rollers and gua sha tools are commonly sold in jade, rose quartz, other stones and stainless steel. We haven't found reliable evidence that one stone type works better than another. Practical differences matter more:

- **Stone** varies in weight and finish and can chip, crack or break if dropped on a hard bathroom floor.
- **Stainless steel** is durable and easy to clean.
- **Material claims vary.** Stone names are sometimes used loosely; if the material matters to you, ask the seller what it is and how it's verified.

## How to use either tool gently

Whichever you choose, the basics are the same. Start on clean skin with a product that lets the tool glide rather than drag, and use light pressure. The American Academy of Dermatology warns that [scrubbing irritates the skin](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/care/face-washing-101), and the same caution applies to pressing hard with a tool. Avoid using tools over broken, irritated or inflamed skin, and stop if you notice redness or discomfort that doesn't settle.

Choose a roller for simplicity and a gua sha stone for control. Both are low-tech, relatively affordable rituals, but neither has strong evidence of lasting change.
