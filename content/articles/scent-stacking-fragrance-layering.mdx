It's a creative, low-cost way to make the fragrances you already own feel new.

## Easy pairings to try

- **Vanilla + citrus:** warm and bright.
- **Musk + rose or jasmine:** soft and romantic.
- **Sandalwood + fig:** creamy and green.
- **Amber + tea:** cozy but fresh.

## Protect your skin

Fragrance ingredients are among the more common causes of allergic skin reactions — a [clinical review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14572300/) estimated that a few percent of people are sensitized to common fragrance ingredients. The FDA notes that fragrance blends can be listed simply as "fragrance," and that products labeled ["unscented" can still contain masking fragrance](https://fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/fragrances-cosmetics).

If you notice itching or redness, stop using the product. Spraying fragrance on clothing or hair accessories instead of skin is a common workaround — check that it won't stain the fabric first.

## Common scent-stacking mistakes

- **Layering two strong, competing scents.** Two heavy perfumes rarely blend well; pair a heavy base with a light accent.
- **Over-applying.** Layering means each layer should be lighter, not that you need more of everything.
- **Rubbing your wrists together.** It doesn't help fragrance last and can make it feel muddled.
- **Storing perfume in the bathroom.** Heat, light and humidity can change a fragrance over time.

## The bottom line

Scent stacking is about play, not rules: start with a simple base, add one accent, keep layers few, and test before committing. Protect your skin by stopping anything that irritates and keeping fragrance off broken or sensitive areas.
