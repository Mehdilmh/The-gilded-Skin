Under-eye patches are one of the most popular self-care products online — a 20-minute ritual that looks great in photos. They're genuinely pleasant to use. It just helps to know what they can and can't do.

## What under-eye patches do

Most patches are made of **hydrogel**, a gel that holds water and skincare ingredients against the skin. While they're on, they hydrate and cool the thin skin under the eyes. That can make puffiness and fine dryness lines look less noticeable for a few hours.

They don't change the structure of the under-eye area, so any effect is temporary.

## When to see a doctor

Sudden or one-sided swelling, swelling with pain or itching, or dark circles that change quickly are worth a medical opinion. For long-standing pigmentation or eye bags, a board-certified dermatologist or oculoplastic surgeon can explain the options.

## Common mistakes with under-eye patches

- **Placing them too close to the lash line.** Serum can migrate into the eyes. Leave a small gap.
- **Wearing them too long.** Longer isn't better — follow the brand's timing and remove them before they dry out.
- **Reusing single-use patches.** Reusing them near the eyes isn't hygienic.
- **Expecting permanent change.** Treat them as a temporary refresh before an event or after a short night.

## The bottom line

Under-eye patches are a lovely, temporary refresh: they hydrate and cool, and can make you look a little more awake. Enjoy them as a ritual, but don't expect them to erase dark circles.
