Makeup brushes and sponges touch your face every day, collect oil, dead skin and product, and often live in a warm, humid bathroom. A quick, regular cleaning routine keeps them working well and more hygienic.

## What research has found

A 2025 study of [cosmetic brushes](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12074853/) found bacteria including Staphylococcus and Micrococcus species, as well as Pseudomonas, and reported that many participants rarely cleaned their brushes. The study didn't find a direct link between cleaning frequency and skin problems — but clean tools are a simple, low-cost habit, and the FDA's [cosmetic safety tips](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/resources-consumers-cosmetics/using-cosmetics-safely) recommend keeping tools clean and not sharing makeup.

## Store them well

Once dry, store brushes upright in a clean holder or a covered case, away from the sink. Our [vanity organization guide](/how-to-organize-a-skincare-routine) has ideas for keeping tools tidy.

Clean brushes also blend better — especially with sheer bases like [skin tints](/skin-tint-vs-foundation) and stains like [jelly blush](/jelly-blush-gummy-makeup-trend).

Eye hygiene matters beyond brushes, too: if you have lash lifts or extensions, see our [Korean lash lift guide](/korean-lash-lift-guide) for aftercare questions to ask.

## Common brush-cleaning mistakes

- **Soaking brushes in a cup of water.** Water seeps into the ferrule and loosens the glue, so bristles start to shed.
- **Drying them upright.** Water runs down into the handle. Lay them flat or hang them bristles-down.
- **Using harsh soaps or hot water.** These can dry out natural bristles and warp synthetic ones.
- **Putting damp brushes away.** Closed cases trap moisture; make sure brushes are completely dry first.
- **Forgetting sponges and puffs.** Damp sponges need the most frequent cleaning of all your tools.

## The bottom line

Wash brushes weekly (or every two weeks for powder), clean sponges after every use or two, keep water away from the handle and dry bristles-down. And don't share your brushes.
