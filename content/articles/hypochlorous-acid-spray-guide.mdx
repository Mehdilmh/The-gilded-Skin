Hypochlorous acid (HOCl) sprays have become a fixture of minimalist routines. The ingredient has a genuinely interesting history — and the marketing sometimes gets ahead of the evidence.

## What hypochlorous acid is

Hypochlorous acid is a weak acid that the body's own white blood cells (neutrophils) produce to fight microbes, as described in a [review on its use in acne](https://mdedge.com/content/topical-hypochlorous-acid-acne-vulgaris-mechanisms-clinical-evidence-and-therapeutic-potential). It's been used as a wound disinfectant for more than a century, according to the [Journal of Drugs in Dermatology](https://jddonline.com/articles/hypochlorous-acid-blast-past-S1545961624P1024X/), and today it's used in wound care and eyelid hygiene products.

A 2025 [narrative review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12730738/) discusses possible roles in conditions such as atopic dermatitis, seborrheic dermatitis and acne. Narrative reviews summarize what's been published, but they aren't the same as large randomized trials — and for everyday cosmetic sprays, those trials are mostly missing.

## The bottom line

Hypochlorous acid is a well-understood antimicrobial with real medical uses, and a pleasant, gentle step in a skincare routine. For acne, eczema and rosacea, think of it as supporting cast, not the lead — and take persistent skin problems to a board-certified dermatologist.
