A beautiful vanity is not the same as a well-organized one. The arrangement that looks best in photographs — every bottle on display, lined up in a sunny window — is often the one that makes a routine harder to keep and products harder to protect. The approach below works in reverse: start with how you actually use your skincare, then choose storage that fits.

## Step 1: Edit before you organize

Organizing products you don't use only gives them a nicer home. Before buying a single tray, take everything out and sort it into three piles: use regularly, use occasionally, and haven't touched in months.

While you're sorting, check dates. In the U.S., most cosmetics aren't legally required to carry an expiration date, and FDA describes printed dates as "rules of thumb" rather than guarantees, so look for both an expiration date and the small open-jar symbol (for example, "12M") that shows how long a product is meant to last after opening. Discard anything that has changed in color, smell or texture. Eye-area products deserve extra attention: [FDA notes](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling/shelf-life-and-expiration-dating-cosmetics) that manufacturers usually recommend discarding mascara two to four months after purchase.

<Callout title="A note on sunscreen" tone="tip">
  Sunscreens and other products with drug claims, such as acne treatments, are regulated as drugs in the U.S. and are required to carry expiration dates. Check them during your edit.
</Callout>

## Step 2: Group by routine, not by category

The most useful organizing principle is the order in which you use things. The American Academy of Dermatology suggests [applying products in a certain order](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/care/apply-skin-care-certain-order): cleanser first, then any treatment, then moisturizer and/or sunscreen, then makeup. Store them in that same sequence, left to right, and the routine becomes almost automatic.

A simple grouping that works for most people:

- **Morning:** cleanser, any morning treatment, moisturizer, sunscreen.
- **Evening:** cleanser, evening treatment, moisturizer.
- **Weekly or occasional:** masks, exfoliants, tools you don't use daily.
- **Backstock:** unopened spares and travel sizes.

If a product appears in both routines, give it a single home at the point where the two routines overlap — usually the moisturizer.

## Step 3: Use three storage zones

Once products are grouped, assign each group a zone based on how often you reach for it.

1. **The counter (daily).** Only the products you use every day. A single tray defines the space and stops the collection from creeping across the counter.
2. **The drawer or cabinet (weekly).** Treatments and tools you use a few times a week. Drawer dividers keep them upright and visible.
3. **Elsewhere (backstock).** Unopened products don't need to be in the bathroom at all. A cool, dark closet shelf is a better long-term home.

## Step 4: Protect products from heat and light

FDA's [shelf-life guidance](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/resources-consumers-cosmetics/cosmetics-safety-qa-shelf-life) notes that products stored in high temperatures or exposed to sunlight can deteriorate well before their expiration date. In practice, that means:

- Keep displays away from sunny windowsills and radiators.
- Avoid open shelves right beside the shower, where products are repeatedly warmed by steam.
- Close caps and pumps fully after each use.
- Follow any storage instructions printed on the label, such as "store in a cool, dry place."

For a small bathroom, vertical storage usually beats horizontal: a slim riser or a wall shelf away from the shower frees counter space without hiding what you use daily. Always measure the space — width, depth and the clearance under any mirror cabinet — before ordering.

## The bottom line

Start by editing, group by the order you apply products, and store each group according to how often you use it. Choose organizers last, after you know exactly what needs a home, and keep everything away from heat, steam and direct sunlight.
