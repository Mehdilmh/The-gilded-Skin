## Plate width and length

Narrow plates (around 1 inch) are versatile, work on short hair and bangs, and make it easier to curl with a straightener. Longer plates cover more hair per pass, which helps with long hair.

## Temperature control and safety

Research in the [Journal of Cosmetic Science](https://library.scconline.org/v062n02/55) found heat damage to hair is strongly temperature dependent, so the ability to choose a low setting matters more than a high maximum. Dermatologists interviewed by [WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/beauty/features/how-not-to-wreck-your-hair?page=1) advise keeping the heat moderate, avoiding wet hair, using a protective product and limiting how often you flat-iron.

## How to straighten with less damage

1. Start with completely dry, detangled hair.
2. Apply a heat protectant.
3. Use the lowest setting that works for your hair.
4. Work in small sections with one slow pass rather than several fast ones.
5. Give your hair heat-free days each week.

Prefer curls? Read our guide to [automatic curling irons](/automatic-curling-iron-guide). Traveling with hot tools? A [travel case](/how-to-choose-a-travel-skincare-case) with a heat-resistant pouch keeps everything safe.

## Common straightener mistakes

- **Cranking the heat to maximum.** The highest setting is rarely needed and raises the risk of damage.
- **Multiple fast passes.** One slow, controlled pass on a small section usually works better than several quick ones.
- **Straightening dirty hair.** Product build-up can sizzle and transfer to the plates. Start with clean, dry hair.
- **Not cleaning the plates.** Wipe cooled plates with a soft, slightly damp cloth to remove residue.
- **Storing it hot.** Let it cool completely on a heat-resistant surface before packing it away.

## The bottom line

Choose plates for your hair type, a width for your length and — above all — adjustable heat with a low starting point and auto shut-off. Then use the lowest temperature that works, protect your hair and keep heat-styling to a few days a week.
