A small bathroom vanity has to do a lot: hold the things you use every day, hide everything else and still leave room to actually wash your face. Most clutter problems come from storing too much on the counter and too little vertically. These ideas work for single-sink vanities, pedestal sinks with a shelf and rental bathrooms where you can't drill.

## Start by editing, then measuring

Organizers can't fix a shelf that holds three half-used moisturizers. Before buying anything:

1. **Pull everything out** of the cabinet and off the counter.
2. **Toss what's expired, separated, smells off or you haven't used** in months. Our guide to [organizing a skincare routine](/how-to-organize-a-skincare-routine) walks through editing by morning, evening and weekly use.
3. **Sort what's left** into daily, weekly and backup or occasional items.
4. **Measure.** Note the cabinet's interior width, depth and height, the space on each side of the pipes, the clearance between shelf edge and door, and the depth of the counter behind the faucet.

Measuring is the step most people skip, and it's the reason so many organizers end up returned. Cabinet doors often have a frame that's narrower than the interior; pipes eat into the middle; and a drawer organizer that's a centimeter too tall won't close.

A single tray creates a boundary: when it's full, something has to go back in the cabinet. It also makes wiping the counter a ten-second job. Choose something with a lip so bottles don't slide, and avoid open cups that collect water at the bottom.

## Go vertical

Height is the most underused space in a small vanity.

- **Stackable drawers or risers** inside the cabinet double usable shelf space. Clear fronts let you see what's inside without unstacking.
- **A two-tier sliding organizer** under the sink lets you reach the back without emptying the front.
- **Turntables** work well in deep corners for tall bottles.
- **A narrow wall shelf** above the faucet or beside the mirror holds a few daily items, as long as it isn't right in the shower's splash zone.

## Use the cabinet door and under-sink space

The inside of a vanity door is free real estate. Adhesive hooks can hold a hair dryer cord or a tool pouch; slim pockets or racks suit cotton pads, sheet masks and small tubes. Check that whatever you attach still lets the door close past the shelf edge — this is where your measurements matter.

Under the sink, work around the pipes rather than against them. Narrow bins fit on either side, and U-shaped organizers wrap around the drain. Leave the shut-off valves reachable. If you keep cleaning products here, put them in their own bin, separate from skincare, and see the safety section below if children live in or visit your home.

## Heat, humidity and product storage

A small bathroom gets steamy fast, and the sunniest shelf is often the worst place for products. FDA notes that expiration dates are rules of thumb and that products [exposed to high heat or sunlight may deteriorate](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/resources-consumers-cosmetics/cosmetics-safety-qa-shelf-life) before that date. FDA also advises [not storing cosmetics above 85°F](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-products/eye-cosmetic-safety).

## Keep products safely away from children

In a small bathroom, everything tends to end up at a child's eye level. That matters: a Utah Poison Control physician told KSL that [cosmetics and personal care items are the most common poisoning category](https://www.ksl.com/article/18563237/cosmetics-are-most-common-cause-of-poisoning-in-utah) in children under 6. Poison Control advises [keeping products in their original containers](https://poisoncontrol.org/prevention/) and stored out of children's reach, and the CPSC recommends [cabinets with child safety latches or locks](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/382.pdf) for household products.

- Store nail polish remover, fragrance, alcohol-based products and cleaning supplies high up and latched, not under an unlocked sink.
- Keep everything in its original, labeled container — don't decant cleaners or removers into cups or bottles that look like drinks.
- Put products away straight after use rather than leaving them on the counter.
- Save the Poison Control number, 1-800-222-1222, in your phone.

## The bottom line

Edit first, measure second, then buy. Keep one tray of daily essentials on the counter, go vertical inside the cabinet, use the door and the space around the pipes, and move backups and heat-sensitive products somewhere cooler. If children are around, store anything risky up high, latched and in its original container.
