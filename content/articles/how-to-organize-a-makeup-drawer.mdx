A makeup drawer tends to collect everything: the foundation you wear daily, three nearly identical nude lipsticks, a palette from years ago and a tangle of brushes. Organizing it isn't really about buying the perfect insert. It's about deciding what deserves the space, then giving each item a spot you can reach without thinking.

## Step 1: Empty it and edit

Take everything out and wipe the drawer. Then sort products into three piles: keep, toss and unsure.

FDA notes that there's no U.S. law requiring expiration dates on cosmetics, so most dating is left to manufacturers. Its [shelf-life guidance](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling/shelf-life-and-expiration-dating-cosmetics) points out that manufacturers usually recommend discarding mascara two to four months after purchase, because it's exposed to bacteria and fungi every time you use it. FDA also describes expiration dates as [rules of thumb](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/resources-consumers-cosmetics/cosmetics-safety-qa-shelf-life): a product stored badly can go off sooner.

For the "unsure" pile, try each item in the next week. Anything you don't reach for can go.

## Step 2: Group by routine and frequency

Sorting by product type (all lipsticks together, all brushes together) is tidy, but it isn't always practical. Grouping by how you actually use things usually works better:

- **Daily kit:** The handful of products in your everyday routine. If you follow something like our [day makeup look](/day-makeup-look), that might be a base, concealer, blush, brow gel, mascara and a lip.
- **Weekly or occasional:** Bolder lip colors, palettes, highlighter, eyeliner.
- **Tools:** Brushes, sponges, lash curler, sharpener, tweezers.
- **Backstock:** Unopened spares. These don't need prime drawer space; a cool, dry closet shelf is fine.

## Step 3: Measure the drawer

This is the step most people skip, and it's the reason so many organizers end up returned. Measure the **inside** of the drawer, not the outside of the cabinet.

## Step 5: Upright or flat?

Different products store best in different positions:

- **Upright:** Liquid foundation, concealer and primer bottles, with caps tightly closed. Mascara and liners can stand in a narrow cup or lie in a slot.
- **On their sides in slots:** Lipsticks and pencils, with the shade or label facing up so you can find them quickly.
- **Flat:** Compacts, palettes and pressed powders, stacked no more than two or three high so you're not digging.

## Storing brushes and tools

Brushes need two things: dryness and protection for the bristles. After washing, let them dry completely before they go back in a drawer. e.l.f.'s [brush-cleaning guide](https://elfcosmetics.com/blogs/the-elf-word/how-to-clean-your-makeup-brushes) cautions against putting brushes in closed containers while still wet, which can slow drying and encourage mold. Our guide to [cleaning makeup brushes](/how-to-clean-makeup-brushes) covers how often to wash them.

In the drawer, lay brushes in a dedicated compartment, or use a brush roll or covered case so bristles don't get bent or pick up powder from other products. Keep sponges in a ventilated spot rather than a sealed box.

Keep your lash curler and tweezers clean and separate from loose powders. FDA advises keeping anything used near the eyes clean and [not sharing eye cosmetics](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-products/eye-cosmetic-safety). If you use a heated curler, store it cool and capped; see our [heated eyelash curler guide](/heated-eyelash-curler-guide).

## Mind the heat

Where the drawer is matters as much as what's in it. FDA notes that temperature changes and sunlight can alter a product's color, texture and smell, and that products stored at high temperatures may deteriorate before their date. Avoid drawers beside radiators, under sunny windows or right next to a steamy shower. The same principles apply to skincare; our guide to [organizing skincare products](/how-to-organize-a-skincare-routine) uses a similar zone approach for your counter and cabinet.

## The bottom line

Organize a makeup drawer in order: edit first, group by routine and frequency, measure the inside of the drawer, then buy dividers that fit. Keep the daily kit at the front, store brushes only when fully dry, replace mascara every few months and keep the whole drawer away from heat and steam.
