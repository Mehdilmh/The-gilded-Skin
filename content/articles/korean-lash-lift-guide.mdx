"Korean lash lift" has become one of the fastest-rising beauty searches of 2026 — trade publication [Happi](https://www.happi.com/breaking-news/korean-lash-lift-azelaic-acid-lead-2026s-most-in-demand-beauty-trends/) reported an enormous year-over-year jump in U.S. searches. Before you book, here's what the treatment involves and what eye doctors want you to know.

## What a Korean lash lift is

A lash lift works like a perm for your natural lashes: a technician shapes your lashes over a small silicone pad, then applies a solution that softens and resets their shape, followed by a neutralizer. A **Korean lash lift** is generally styled to lift lashes from the root for a straighter, more upright, "lifted" look rather than a strong curl. Results typically last several weeks, until the lashes shed and regrow.

## The risks to know

An ophthalmologist interviewed by [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/are-lash-lifts-safe) points to allergic reactions and notes that repeated intense lash treatments can make people more prone to blepharitis (eyelid inflammation). [All About Vision](https://www.allaboutvision.com/eye-care/cosmetic/eyelash-lift/) lists chemical burns or corneal injury if the perming solution gets into the eye. The FDA's [cosmetic safety guidance](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/resources-consumers-cosmetics/using-cosmetics-safely) also advises against dyeing or tinting lashes and brows at home.

## Salon vs. at-home kits

At-home kits are widely sold, but applying perming solution millimeters from your own eye is hard to do precisely. A trained, licensed technician working on you with your eyes closed is the safer choice.

## Who should talk to an eye doctor first

If you have dry eye, eye allergies, frequent eye infections, blepharitis or very sensitive eyes — or if you wear contact lenses — check with your eye doctor before booking. Seek prompt medical care if you have eye pain, vision changes or persistent redness after a treatment.

## Aftercare

The Cleveland Clinic ophthalmologist recommends keeping lashes clean after a lift rather than skipping cleansing. Follow your technician's timing for getting lashes wet, avoid rubbing your eyes, and use a gentle, eye-safe cleanser. Keep tools clean, too — see [how to clean makeup brushes](/how-to-clean-makeup-brushes).

## The bottom line

A Korean lash lift can give a lifted, low-maintenance look for several weeks, but it's a chemical treatment near your eyes.
