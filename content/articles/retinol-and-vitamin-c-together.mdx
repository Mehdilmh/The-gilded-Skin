Retinol and vitamin C are two of the most popular "active" ingredients, and a common question is whether they can be used together. They can — the trick is timing and patience.

A [review of retinoids for skin aging](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2699641/) describes tretinoin as the most widely investigated retinoid for photoaging, with retinol and retinaldehyde being considerably less irritating. For vitamin C, a [dermatology review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5605218/) discusses its antioxidant and photoprotective roles but notes that clinical studies of topical formulations remain limited.

Because retinoids can make skin more sensitive to the sun, daily sunscreen is non-negotiable — the AAD recommends [broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher](https://www.aad.org/media/stats/prevention-and-care/sunscreen-faqs). ## If your skin gets irritated

Some dryness and flaking is common when starting retinol. Cut back to fewer nights, buffer with moisturizer, and see the habits in our [cold-weather skincare guide](/cold-weather-skin-barrier-routine). If you also use [azelaic acid](/what-is-azelaic-acid), keep it on alternate nights at first. Tempted by newer "regenerative" serums? Read our [PDRN explainer](/pdrn-skincare-explained) before adding another active.

## Who should check with a doctor first

Talk to a doctor before using retinoids if you're pregnant, trying to conceive or breastfeeding, or if you have rosacea, eczema or very sensitive skin. A dermatologist can also tell you whether prescription tretinoin makes sense for you.

## Common mistakes with retinol and vitamin C

- **Starting both at once.** If your skin reacts, you won't know which one caused it.
- **Using too much retinol.** A pea-sized amount covers the whole face; more just adds irritation.
- **Skipping sunscreen.** Retinoids make skin more sun-sensitive, and vitamin C is not a substitute for SPF.
- **Storing vitamin C badly.** Some forms break down with light, heat and air. Keep the cap closed and follow the storage instructions.
- **Giving up too soon.** Retinoids take months, not weeks.

## The bottom line

You can absolutely use retinol and vitamin C — just split them: vitamin C in the morning under sunscreen, retinol at night. Start slowly, add one thing at a time, and keep moisturizer and SPF as your constants.
