A good day makeup look is mostly about restraint: even skin, a little warmth, defined lashes and comfortable lips, in products light enough to wear for hours. The order matters more than the number of products. Below is a routine you can do in about five minutes, a 10-minute version for days you want a little more, and adjustments for oily or acne-prone skin.

## Step 1: Skincare and sunscreen first

Start with clean skin and a moisturizer if you need one, then sunscreen. The American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) advises applying sunscreen [after your other skincare and beneath your makeup](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/care/skin-care-in-your-20s), and notes that makeup with SPF isn't a reliable substitute: you'd need SPF 30 or higher, applied generously and reapplied every two hours. Use a broad-spectrum SPF 30+ sunscreen and give it a few minutes to settle before your base.

Our [simple morning skincare routine](/how-to-build-a-simple-morning-skincare-routine) covers the skincare steps in more detail.

## Step 2: A light base, only where you need it

For daytime, a sheer base usually looks more natural than full coverage everywhere. A skin tint or a thin layer of foundation evens tone; concealer handles specific spots such as redness around the nose or under-eye shadows. Many people find they only need concealer. If you're unsure which base suits you, see [skin tint vs. foundation](/skin-tint-vs-foundation).

Apply base with fingertips, a damp sponge or a brush, working from the center of the face outward and sheering it out toward the hairline and jaw.

## Step 3: Cheeks

A cream or liquid blush is the fastest way to make a light base look fresh. Dot a little on the apples of the cheeks and blend upward toward the temples with fingertips. Cream textures are forgiving because you can build them gradually; trends such as [jelly blush](/jelly-blush-gummy-makeup-trend) work on the same principle. If you prefer powder blush, apply it after any setting powder.

## Step 4: Brows, eyes and lips

- **Brows:** Brush them up and into place with a spoolie or clear brow gel. Fill sparse areas lightly with a pencil or powder only if you want more definition.
- **Eyes:** One coat of mascara, wiggling from the root. For a little more depth, sweep a neutral matte shadow close to the lash line. FDA advises [not sharing eye cosmetics](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-products/eye-cosmetic-safety), keeping applicators clean and never applying mascara in a moving vehicle.
- **Lips:** A tinted balm, sheer lipstick or gloss. Our guide to [lip gloss vs. lip oil](/lip-gloss-vs-lip-oil) compares the comfortable options.

## End of the day

Remove makeup before bed, including eye makeup, and then wash with a gentle cleanser; the AAD notes that even non-comedogenic makeup can contribute to breakouts if you sleep in it. Clean tools matter too — see our guide to [cleaning makeup brushes](/how-to-clean-makeup-brushes). Replace mascara regularly; FDA notes that manufacturers usually recommend discarding it [two to four months after purchase](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-labeling/shelf-life-and-expiration-dating-cosmetics).

## The bottom line

A natural day look is sunscreen, a light base where you need it, a touch of cream blush, groomed brows, mascara and a comfortable lip. Start with the five-minute version, add steps only when they earn their place, and remove everything before bed.
