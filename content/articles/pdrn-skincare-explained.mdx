PDRN has gone from Korean clinics to everyday serums, usually marketed as "salmon DNA." The science behind it is real — but most of it doesn't come from the kind of product you can buy off a shelf.

## What PDRN is

PDRN stands for **polydeoxyribonucleotide**, a mixture of DNA fragments most often extracted from salmon. Researchers describe it as acting on adenosine A2A receptors, which are involved in tissue repair, according to a [narrative review](https://brieflands.com/journals/jssc/articles/159728).

## Where the evidence comes from

This is the key point for shoppers. Much of the encouraging data on PDRN comes from **injected** treatments, procedures and laboratory or animal studies — not from people applying a serum at home. A recent [scoping review of nucleotide-based skin boosters](https://doi.org/10.1002/der2.70080) concluded that their safety and efficacy are not yet well supported by the available data, despite growing use.

## Should you try a PDRN serum?

If a PDRN product appeals to you, it's reasonable to try — as long as you see it as an experiment. Any comfort or glow you notice may come from the rest of the formula, such as humectants and soothing ingredients.

## The bottom line

PDRN is a genuinely interesting ingredient, but the hype has run ahead of the evidence for at-home serums. Enjoy one if you like it, keep your expectations modest, and don't let it replace the routine basics that are proven: gentle cleansing, moisturizer and daily sunscreen.
