Fall 2026's skincare conversation has shifted from "more actives" to **barrier recovery**: gentler routines, comforting textures and long-term resilience. That's good news for dry, cold-weather skin, because the basics that help most are simple.

## Why skin gets drier in cold weather

Cold outdoor air and indoor heating both lower humidity, and long hot showers strip away the skin's natural oils. The result is the familiar tightness, flaking and itch. The fix isn't a ten-step routine — it's protecting moisture you already have.

## The cold-weather routine, step by step

The American Academy of Dermatology's [dry skin tips](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/dry/dermatologists-tips-relieve-dry-skin) form the backbone of this routine.

The AAD notes that thicker creams and ointments are generally more effective than lotions at holding in moisture.

## Pause the harsh stuff

If your skin stings, flakes or feels raw, ease off exfoliating acids, scrubs and strong retinoids until it settles. Trending actives like [azelaic acid](/what-is-azelaic-acid) are often well tolerated, but even gentle actives are easier to use on a calm, well-moisturized barrier. A soothing mist such as [hypochlorous acid](/hypochlorous-acid-spray-guide) can be a gentle extra step.

## Don't skip sunscreen

UV exposure continues in fall and winter. The AAD recommends [broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher](https://www.aad.org/media/stats/prevention-and-care/sunscreen-faqs) on skin that clothing won't cover. Not sure which type to choose? Read [mineral vs. chemical sunscreen](/mineral-vs-chemical-sunscreen).

## If you shave

Shaving adds friction to skin that is already dry in cold weather. Soften hair first, use a moisturizing shaving cream and shave with the grain — our guide to [preventing razor bumps](/how-to-prevent-razor-bumps) covers the full routine.

## The bottom line

Cold-weather skin needs less, not more: shorter warm showers, gentle cleansing, moisturizer on damp skin, a richer texture where you're dry, and daily sunscreen. If dryness cracks, bleeds or itches persistently, see a board-certified dermatologist — it may be eczema or another condition that needs treatment.
