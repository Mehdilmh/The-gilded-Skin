Packing skincare for a carry-on is mostly a sorting exercise: work out what TSA counts as a liquid, fit those items into one small bag, and move everything else into solid form or your checked luggage. Here's how the rules apply to a typical routine, plus a few habits that keep decanted products clean and leak-free.

## The 3-1-1 rule in plain terms

TSA's [liquids, aerosols and gels rule](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/liquids-aerosols-gels-rule) is usually summarized as 3-1-1:

- **3** — each container holds 3.4 ounces (100 milliliters) or less.
- **1** — all of those containers fit in one quart-sized bag.
- **1** — one bag per passenger.

The limit is about the container's size, not how much is left inside, so a nearly empty full-size bottle still doesn't qualify. Anything larger needs to go in checked baggage. TSA also notes that the officer at the checkpoint makes the final call on whether an item is allowed.

## What counts as a liquid in your skincare bag

The rule isn't just for water-thin products. TSA's guidance covers liquids, aerosols, gels, creams and pastes — so most of a skincare routine falls under it.

Sunscreen catches a lot of people out. In a [2021 statement](https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/statements/2021/04/12/statement-regarding-sunscreen-carry-bags), TSA clarified that sunscreen is subject to the liquids rule and that travelers who need larger amounts should put them in checked luggage. If you're heading somewhere sunny, a 3.4 oz tube in your bag plus a full-size bottle in a checked suitcase is a practical split. Our guide to [mineral vs. chemical sunscreen](/mineral-vs-chemical-sunscreen) can help you pick a formula worth carrying.

## Solid swaps that free up bag space

Solids aren't covered by 3-1-1. TSA lists [bar soap](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/whatcanibring/items/soap-bar) as allowed in carry-ons, and a TSA blog post on deodorant explains that [solid sticks aren't restricted](https://www.tsa.gov/blog/2010/09/03/gel-aerosol-and-stick-deodorant-which-can-i-take-my-carryon-luggage), while gel, roll-on and spray versions are.

Swaps that may work for skincare:

- A bar or solid cleanser instead of a gel or foam cleanser.
- Stick or balm moisturizers and lip balms instead of creams in jars.
- Powder or pressed makeup instead of liquid formulas.

A solid product only helps if your skin likes it, so try any new swap at home a week or two before the trip rather than on the first night away. If you're prone to reactions, our [patch-testing guide](/how-to-patch-test-skincare) walks through how.

## Leak-proof the bag

Changes in cabin pressure can push product out of loosely closed containers. To reduce the mess:

- Fill bottles only about three-quarters full and squeeze out a little air before closing.
- Put a small piece of plastic wrap under the cap of anything without a seal.
- Stand bottles upright in your quart bag where you can, and keep it at the top of your carry-on so you can lift it out at screening.
- Keep anything that could stain, like foundation or self-tanner, in its own small zip bag inside the quart bag.

A dedicated case helps too. Our guide to [choosing a travel skincare case](/how-to-choose-a-travel-skincare-case) covers which features matter, from wipeable linings to compartments that keep bottles upright.

## Medically necessary liquids

Prescription creams and other medically necessary liquids are treated differently. TSA's [medication guidance](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/frequently-asked-questions/i-am-traveling-medication-are-there-any-requirements-i-should-be) allows them in amounts over 3.4 ounces in reasonable quantities for your trip, but you need to tell the officer at the start of screening and remove them from your bag. They'll be screened separately and may get additional screening, which can include opening containers.

Keep prescription products in their original labeled packaging where possible. Cosmetic sunscreen isn't covered by this exception, per TSA's 2021 statement. If you're unsure whether something qualifies, check TSA's "What Can I Bring?" tool before you fly.

## The bottom line

For a carry-on, keep every cream, gel, serum, sunscreen and liquid makeup product at 3.4 ounces or less and fit them in one quart-sized bag. Move what you can to solid formulas, decant into clean, labeled containers, leave a little air space to prevent leaks, and declare any medically necessary liquids at screening. Anything that doesn't fit belongs in checked luggage.
