Razor bumps — small, sometimes painful bumps after shaving — are especially common in people with curly or coarse hair. The good news: a few changes to how you shave make a big difference.

## Why razor bumps happen

When a hair is cut very close, its sharp tip can curl back and grow into the skin, causing inflammation and a bump. Shaving against the grain, stretching the skin and using dull blades all make this more likely.

## How to prevent razor bumps

These tips come from the American Academy of Dermatology's [razor bump prevention guide](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/hair/razor-bump-prevention).

The AAD's [men's shaving tips](https://www.newswise.com/articles/view/689437/?sc=dwhn) add that some people do better with a single- or two-blade razor, and suggest replacing disposable razors after about five to seven shaves.

## Aftercare

Rinse with cool water, pat dry and apply a gentle, fragrance-free moisturizer. In winter, the habits in our [cold-weather skincare guide](/cold-weather-skin-barrier-routine) help keep skin calm.

## When to see a dermatologist

If bumps keep coming back after you've changed your routine, get help. The AAD warns that [untreated razor bumps can leave lasting marks](https://aad.org/news/how-to-prevent-razor-bumps), and a dermatologist can recommend treatments or hair-removal alternatives.

## Common shaving mistakes

- **Shaving dry or after a quick splash of water.** Hair needs a few minutes of warm water to soften.
- **Pressing hard.** Pressure makes the razor cut too close; let the blade do the work.
- **Going over the same area repeatedly.** Each pass adds irritation — rinse and re-lather instead.
- **Keeping blades too long.** Dull blades tug at hair and irritate skin.
- **Storing the razor wet in the shower.** Dry it between uses to keep it cleaner and sharper.

## The bottom line

Soften, protect, shave with the grain, don't stretch your skin, keep blades fresh and shave a little less often. If blades keep causing trouble, an electric or rotary shaver is worth a try.
