Mineral sunscreen is having a moment, especially among people with sensitive skin. But "mineral vs. chemical" is less of a battle than the marketing suggests — both protect you when they're used correctly.

## The one rule that matters most

The American Academy of Dermatology recommends a [broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher](https://www.aad.org/media/stats/prevention-and-care/sunscreen-faqs), applied generously and reapplied about every two hours outdoors. Any sunscreen that meets that bar and that you'll wear daily is a good choice.

In its [2019 proposed rule](https://www.cov.com/-/media/files/corporate/publications/2019/02/fda_issues_new_proposed_rule_on_regulatory_requirements_for_sunscreen_products.pdf), FDA proposed that zinc oxide and titanium dioxide are generally recognized as safe and effective, and said it needed more data for many chemical filters. "More data needed" is not the same as "unsafe," and chemical sunscreens remain widely used.

## Sensitive and rosacea-prone skin

The [National Rosacea Society](https://www.rosacea.org/blog/2026/june/understanding-your-sunscreen-options) notes that some people with rosacea find mineral sunscreen more tolerable — but that there's no hard rule, and some sensitive skin reacts to mineral formulas too. Fragrance-free formulas are a sensible starting point.

## The bottom line

Choose mineral if your skin is sensitive or you prefer zinc oxide; choose chemical or hybrid if you want the lightest feel. Either way, broad spectrum, SPF 30+, generous application and daily use matter far more than the filter type.
